The Complete Coffee Types Guide

From intense Italian espressos to creamy modern classics — learn what’s really inside your cup.

Nearly every coffee drink you see in cafés around the world is built on espresso. The differences come down to three things: coffee dose, extraction style, and milk or water ratios.

Below is a practical, no-nonsense guide to the most common coffee types, where they come from, how they're prepared, and what you can expect in terms of strength and flavor.

Espresso-Based Coffees (No Milk)

Ristretto

Origin: Italy

A ristretto is a shortened espresso extraction. Using the same amount of ground coffee as a normal espresso but less water, it produces a smaller, sweeter, and more concentrated shot.

  • Grind: Finer than espresso
  • Ground coffee: 7–9 g
  • Volume: ~15–20 ml
  • Extraction time: ~25 seconds
  • Cup: espresso cup (60 ml)
  • Caffeine: ~40–70 mg
  • Flavor characteristics: Sweeter, thicker, less bitter; more syrupy and intense

There are multiple techniques around the world and they all result in completely different coffees with only one thing in common — less volume than espresso.

How to prepare:

  • Method 1 (fine grind method): Use finer grind with standard espresso dose and extraction time. The provided volume will be less because of the finer grind.
  • Method 2 (shorter time method): Use same grind as espressobut extract for 15–20 seconds instead of 25–30 seconds.
  • Method 3 (more coffee method): Increase dose (e.g., 15 g) with same grind and time as espresso, resulting in lower output volume.

Espresso

Origin: Italy

Espresso is brewed by forcing hot water through finely ground coffee under pressure. It is concentrated, intense, and forms the base of most coffee drinks.

  • Ground coffee: 7–9 g
  • Volume: ~25–30 ml
  • Cup: espresso cup (60–90 ml)
  • Caffeine: ~60–100 mg
  • Pressure: ~9 bars
  • Water temperature: 92°C–96°C (197°F–205°F)
  • Flavor characteristics: Balanced sweetness, acidity, and bitterness with moderate body

How to prepare: use grinding for espresso, and then it's all pretty simple on standard coffee machines: 7–9 g coffee in, ~30 ml out, 25–30 seconds.

Doppio

Origin: Italy

A doppio is a double espresso — two shots extracted together. In many modern cafés, this is the default espresso size.

  • Ground coffee: 14–20 g
  • Volume: ~60 ml
  • Cup: small ceramic cup (120 ml)
  • Caffeine: ~120–200 mg

How to prepare: Doppio is basically just two espresso caffes. To prepare a doppio, use grinding for espresso. On standard coffee machines use handle for 2 shots, add there 14-20 g of coffee and pour both shots at the same time into one cup: 14-20 g coffee in, ~60 ml out, 25–30 seconds.

Lungo

Origin: Italy

A lungo is extracted with more water during brewing process. The longer extraction gives the espresso a milder and less concentrated flavour. It may look about the same size as a doppio, but the difference is that a lungo does not have that much caffeine and is much more watered down with double the amount of hot water than a regular espresso shot. Has slightly more coffein than a regular espresso but since coffein is extracted in the begining of the extraction the difference is not that big.

  • Ground coffee: 7–9 g
  • Volume: ~60 ml
  • Cup: small coffee cup (90–120 ml)
  • Caffeine: ~70–100 mg

How to prepare: Lungo is prepared just like a regular espresso but with more water - longer extraction time. To prepare use grinding for espresso, and then use more water than usual - extract for twice the time of a regular espresso: 7-9 g coffee, ~60 ml out, 50–60 seconds.

Americano

Origin: Italy / United States

An Americano is espresso diluted with hot water after brewing. This preserves espresso flavor while reducing intensity.

  • Ground coffee: 7–9 g
  • Volume: ~25–30 ml
  • Cup: espresso cup (60-90 ml)
  • Caffeine: ~60–100 mg
  • Water added: ~90–120 ml

How to prepare: prepare standard espresso and then add as much hot water as needed to reach the desired volume.

Milk-Based Coffee Drinks

Cappuccino

Origin: Italy

A cappuccino consists of equal parts espresso, steamed milk, and milk foam. Traditionally consumed in the morning in Italy. The foam should be thick creamy texture (about 1–2 cm of foam) unlike thin microfoam as in latte. This is a common misconception that cappuccinos should have microfoam. In reality, traditional cappuccinos have a much thicker and more stable foam layer than lattes.

  • Espresso: 1/3 of cup volume
  • Milk: 1/3 of cup volume
  • Foam: 1/3 of cup volume

How to prepare: prepare standard espresso then add steamed milk and top with a thick layer of milk foam. The ideal ratio is 1/3 espresso, 1/3 steamed milk, and 1/3 milk foam. The foam should be thick and creamy, not thin microfoam.

Flat White

Origin: Australia / New Zealand

A flat white uses a double espresso and finely textured milk with minimal foam, emphasizing coffee flavor. The ratio is typically around 1/3 espresso to 2/3 milk, with a very thin layer of microfoam on top. Microfoam on top of a flat white should be no more than 0.5 cm thick, and it should have a glossy, velvety appearance without large bubbles.

  • Espresso (2 shots): 1/3 of cup volume
  • Milk: 2/3 of cup volume
  • Foam: very thin layer (max 0.5 cm thick)

How to prepare: prepare double espresso then add steamed milk and top with a thin layer of milk foam. The ideal ratio is 1/3 espresso, 2/3 steamed milk topped with a thin layer of microfoam. And if you want and can you can play with a bit of latte art on top of the flat white since it has that thin layer of microfoam.

Latte

Origin: Italy (modern style globalized)

"Latte" is short for "caffè latte," which means "milk coffee" in Italian.

A latte contains more milk than a cappuccino, resulting in a smooth and mild drink with a thin foam layer. Typically latte is made of 1/4 espresso and 3/4 steamed milk with a thin layer of microfoam on top. The microfoam on top of a latte should be around 0.5 cm thick.

  • Espresso: 1/4 of cup volume
  • Milk: 3/4 of cup volume
  • Foam: very thin layer

How to prepare: prepare single espresso then add steamed milk and top with a thin layer of milk foam. The ideal ratio is 1/4 espresso, 3/4 steamed milk topped with a thin layer of microfoam. And if you want and can you can play with a bit of latte art on top since its called "latte" art.

Cortado / Gibraltar

Origin: Spain

  • Espresso: 1/2 of cup volume
  • Milk: 1/2 of cup volume
  • Foam: None

Equal parts espresso and warm milk. Strong, balanced, and no foam. Typically made with a single espresso shot and an equal amount of steamed milk with very little to no foam.
